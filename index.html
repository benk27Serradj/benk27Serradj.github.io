Right now I'm an apprentice at Renault, where I'm turning research-grade speech and language models into something that runs in real time on actual car hardware. Experience

Embedded AI Engineer · Apprenticeship Sep. 2025 – Sep. 2027 (ongoing)

Ampere Software Technology (Renault Group)

I'm part of an innovation R&D team at Renault focused on the next-generation cockpit. The team works mainly on cloud-based and embedded voice assistants, and I focus on the embedded side, evolving from Software-Defined Vehicle (SDV) architecture to what comes next, AI-Defined Vehicle (AIDV), on hardware that isn't in production cars yet.

I started by comparing models instead of picking one on instinct: text-to-speech and speech-recognition models, benchmarked directly against the target hardware rather than a GPU in a lab, alongside research into candidate language models rather than a formal benchmark. A model that sounds great on my laptop can be useless on hardware a fraction of the size. Most of them were too slow or too heavy. A handful weren't.

The real work was designing the assistant's software architecture end to end: assembling ASR, LLM, and TTS models into a single pipeline, applying compression where needed (quantization, etc.), and deploying through the right inference engine, with C++ and cross-compilation for on-device execution. That architecture is what carries over, regardless of which specific model ends up shipping.

Beyond the models themselves, I built what makes it feel like a real voice assistant: audio streaming, voice activity detection, half-duplex handling, echo cancellation, and end-of-conversation detection, along with system prompts, fine-tuning, and hyperparameter selection to shape how it behaves, and tool calling so it can actually control the car: temperature, volume, navigation, media, and more.

I also explored emotion-conditioning for more expressive TTS, and wrote deployment guides covering architecture, inference engine, and benchmark results for every model evaluated, so the work could be reused by the rest of the team.

All of it connects through a Kotlin/Android service I built that acts as a proxy between the assistant and Renault's in-car application, the same way a cloud provider would plug in. That first version is deployed and tested today in a vehicle fitted with this future hardware, handling real conversations and tool calls, not a demo on a laptop.
